# Brand Voice & Style Guide

## Positioning

Ronning Systems, LLC is a technology consultancy and the company behind **Joblign** — a job application tracking system with AI-powered resume generation and ATS analysis. The site leads with the product (Joblign) and presents consulting as a secondary offering.

## Voice

- **Practical, not hypey.** We describe what things do and why they matter. No "revolutionary", "game-changing", or "cutting-edge".
- **Direct and concrete.** Prefer specific outcomes ("orbit insertion on first flight", "closure of 3 enterprise risks") over vague claims ("world-class quality").
- **Confident but honest.** We state what we've done and what we're building. Where something is in progress (e.g. Joblign plan pricing), we say so plainly.
- **Human.** First-person where it's Patrick speaking ("I step in as your fractional CTO"), "we" for the company.

## Terminology

- The product is **Joblign**, tagline **"Get aligned for success"**.
- The legacy name **JobSync** must never appear in user-visible copy.
- The repo name `job-app` and Docker image `job-app` are internal artifact names, not brand strings — leave them as-is.
- The opaque Auth0 API identifier `https://jobsync/api` is an internal identifier, not a brand string — do not mass-replace.

## Naming

- Company: **Ronning Systems, LLC**
- Product: **Joblign**
- Platform services: **appliances** (lowercase, as in "the appliance catalog")
- Founder: **Patrick Ronning**

## Tone rules

- Use em-dashes sparingly; prefer commas or restructure.
- Avoid exclamation points in body copy.
- Contractions are fine ("don't", "we're").
- Address the reader as "you" in product copy; "we" for the company.

## Style

- Headlines: sentence case (not Title Case).
- Prices: `$6,000 / month`, `$250 / hour`.
- Dates: `August 2026`.
- Acronyms: spell out on first use (e.g. "Software as a Medical Device (SaMD)").
